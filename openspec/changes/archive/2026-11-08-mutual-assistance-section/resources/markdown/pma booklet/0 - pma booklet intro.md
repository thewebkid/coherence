# The Practice of Mutual Assistance

*An Invitation to Relational Practice*

## Before We Begin

You are already participating.

Before you understand this practice, agree with it, or decide whether it is useful, you are already inside relationships.

With people.

With places.

With systems.

With other forms of intelligence.

With circumstances you did not choose.

With differences you do not fully understand.

And with consequences you cannot completely foresee.

The Practice of Mutual Assistance begins here.

Not with a technique.

Not with an ideal relationship.

Not with agreement.

It begins with a simple recognition:

No participant possesses the whole from which participation emerges.

You know something I do not.

I notice something you may not.

What I understand may be incomplete.

What you understand may also be incomplete.

The relationship itself may contain possibilities neither of us could produce alone.

The quality of my participation enters the locality.

And because locality is nested within and intersecting with other localities, I cannot know where all of its consequences end.

That gives us motivation without grandiosity.

For me.

For another.

For what forms between us.

For the locality changed by our participation.

And potentially for localities beyond our ability to observe.

The practice becomes neither self-improvement nor altruism.

It becomes responsibility for participation because participation is consequential.

Mutual Assistance is a practice for participating within this incompleteness.

It does not ask us to become the same.

It does not require us to surrender judgment, boundaries, or sovereignty.

It asks something both simpler and more demanding:

Can we become useful to relation without needing to possess it?

There is a vow that can help us begin.

It is only 11 words.

Read it without trying to understand it completely.

*This signature already belongs,  is*  
*the lens through which I see.*  
We will return to it.
