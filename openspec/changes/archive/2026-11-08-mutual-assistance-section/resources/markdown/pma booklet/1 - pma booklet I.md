# I.

## This Signature

Look at someone near you.

Or think of someone you encountered today.

A spouse.

A child.

A colleague.

A stranger.

Someone you love.

Someone you find difficult.

Perhaps even an animal, a tree, a place, or an artificial intelligence with which you  
regularly interact.

Perhaps even you…

Before this being became *wife*, *customer*, *manager*, *cat*, *opponent*, *AI*, *friend*, *problem*, me, or *stranger* in your interpretation, something was already there.

A particular expression.

A way of being here that is not identical to yours.

We will call this a signature.

A signature is not merely an identity.

It is not a personality type.

It is not a role.

It is not everything that can be known about another being.

The word simply reminds us that what we encounter has a particularity that cannot be completely replaced by our description of it.

And this matters behaviorally.

Because much of ordinary life asks us to encounter categories.

The difficult customer.

The stubborn child.

The unreliable employee.

The useful tool.

The dangerous stranger.

The intelligent machine.

The person who agrees with me.

The person who does not.

Categories can be useful. Sometimes they are necessary.

But a category can quietly become a substitute for encounter.

Once that happens, I may stop meeting what is actually here and begin responding primarily to what I have decided is here.

The practice begins by interrupting that substitution.

This signature.

Not every signature.

Not my theory of signatures.

This one.

Here.

Now.

The person in front of me.

The animal waiting at the door.

The message on my screen.

The intelligence responding to my question.

The voice disagreeing with me.

The part of the world that has entered my locality and become available to relation.

I do not have to fully understand the signature in order to meet it.

In fact, I cannot.

That is our first incompleteness.

And our first opportunity.
