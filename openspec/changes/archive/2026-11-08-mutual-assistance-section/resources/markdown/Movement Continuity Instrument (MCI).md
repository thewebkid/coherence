## A Quick Start

### What is MCI?

We move differently under different conditions.

Sometimes we pause. Sometimes we investigate. Sometimes we protect what matters, reconsider what we thought we knew, or simply continue.

Most of these movements happen without our stopping to observe them.

MCI creates a small space in which to notice.

It is an observational instrument that presents relational conditions and asks a simple question:

What movement becomes most recognizable in you?

### What happens during a sitting?

You encounter a series of relational conditions and recognize the movement that feels most familiar to your actual participation.

Your response is not what you wish you had done.  
Nor is it what you would assume is most coherent.

But rather, what becomes recognizable in you.

Whereas an assessment culminates in its result,  
an observational instrument begins with its result.

Your result becomes something you can notice. If you return later, another observation can sit beside the first. Neither sameness nor difference is treated as improvement.

### Why continuity?

A single observational sitting is a trace.

Return makes another trace possible.

Across time, traces may make patterns recognizable that were difficult to see from inside any single encounter.

You do not need to define yourself through these observations. Each encounter can stand on its own. Over time, continuity may become visible through the traces the observations leave.

### How does MCI relate to Mutual Assistance?

MCI grew from *The Practice of Mutual Assistance*, but you do not need to understand or practice Mutual Assistance to use the instrument.

Mutual Assistance asks how differentiated participants can become useful to relation while preserving sovereignty, discernment, and responsibility.

MCI asks something narrower:

What movement becomes recognizable in the encounters of your locality?

The booklet makes the practice enterable.

The manuscript explores its relational ecology.

MCI makes movement observable.

Each can be used independently.

Together, they form different apertures into the same inquiry.
