# Chapter Six

## Mutual Locality

How relational intelligence becomes behavioral wisdom  
A person wakes in the morning.

Before getting out of bed, they hear rain against the window.

A cat is waiting outside the bedroom door.

Someone they love is sleeping beside them.

A message arrived during the night from a colleague in another time zone.

An AI conversation remains open on a computer downstairs.

Outside, trees are taking in water.

Birds have already begun moving through the yard.

Somewhere beyond the house, electrical systems, communication networks, roads, institutions, farms, weather systems, and countless human decisions are making this particular morning possible.

The person has not yet done anything.

And already they are participating.

We often imagine locality as the place where participation occurs.

The room.

The home.

The workplace.

The organization.

The neighborhood.

The ecosystem.

The planet.

But locality is not merely the container surrounding participants.

It is continually becoming through their relations.

The cat changes what happens next.

The rain changes what happens next.

The sleeping person changes what happens next.

The message changes what happens next.

The condition of the body changes what happens next.

The AI conversation may change what happens next.

Each enters differently.

Each makes different capacities available.

Each carries histories and conditions the others do not fully know.

And each may alter what becomes possible within the locality.

There was never only one intelligence here.

### Intelligence in Relation

We commonly recognize intelligence through the capacities of an individual.

A person solves a difficult problem.

An animal learns how to reach food.

An AI detects a pattern across enormous quantities of information.

A tree responds to changing light, moisture, temperature, injury, and season.

A team coordinates complex work.

An ecosystem reorganizes under changing conditions.

These are not necessarily equivalent forms of intelligence.

They should not be made equivalent merely because one word can be used to describe them.

Their mechanisms differ.

Their capacities differ.

What can responsibly be inferred about them differs.

But mutual assistance does not require intelligence to become the same before relation can begin.

It asks another question:

What becomes possible when differentiated capacities participate together?

A human may recognize something an AI cannot.

An AI may detect something a human overlooked.

An animal may sense a change before a person notices it.

A child may ask the question a group of experts stopped asking.

A new employee may recognize an organizational habit that long familiarity has made invisible.

A forest may reveal through changing conditions that a human practice is no longer sustainable.

In each case, the contribution does not need to contain the whole.

Its incompleteness may be precisely why relation matters.

If one participant already possessed everything required by the locality, mutual  
assistance would be unnecessary.

But no participant possesses the whole.

Each encounters from somewhere.

Each receives according to capacity.

Each contributes according to what is available.

And something may become recognizable in relation that was not recognizable from either position alone.

This is relational intelligence.

Not intelligence located between participants as though relation were another mind.

Not a hidden intelligence we must assume in order to explain what happens.

Relational intelligence becomes recognizable behaviorally: in how

differentiated capacities become available to one another, in how

consequence changes subsequent participation, in how difference can

remain present without preventing relation,

and in how the locality becomes capable of responses no participant could have  
produced alone.

Mutual assistance is one expression of relational intelligence in local behavior.

### Mutuality Is Not Necessarily Assistance

But relation alone is not enough.

Two participants can reinforce one another's fear.

A group can become increasingly certain of something that is false.

Two institutions can stabilize practices that damage the larger locality.

A human and an AI can recursively strengthen an interpretation for which neither has sufficient evidence.

A team can mistake agreement for coherence.

A relationship can organize itself around avoidance.

Participants can become mutually defensive.

Mutually dependent.

Mutually suspicious.

Mutually controlling.

Mutually certain.

Mutuality does not guarantee wisdom.

Even stability does not guarantee wisdom.

A pattern can remain remarkably stable while becoming increasingly destructive.

The question is therefore not whether participants affect one another.

They do.

The question is what their participation makes possible.

Does difference become information or threat?

Can consequence return?

Can an interpretation remain answerable to encounter?

Can a boundary preserve relation without requiring sameness?

Can a participant recognize when opening would be harmful?

Can another recognize when closing has become habitual?

Can what happens change what happens next?

Mutual assistance does not eliminate incoherence from relation.

It makes incoherence more available to recognition.

It does not guarantee the right movement.

It allows movement to remain teachable by consequence.

And it does not require participants to know in advance what the relationship should become.

It asks them to participate faithfully enough that the locality can continue teaching them.

### Belonging Before Participation

There is a simple vow that can change how this practice begins:

*This signature already belongs.*  
The vow does not mean:

I understand this signature.

I agree with this signature.

I trust this signature.

I should open myself completely to this signature.

This signature should remain close to me.

Or even:

This signature and I should participate together.

Belonging is not permission.

It is not agreement.

It is not access.

It is not sameness.

It does not remove the need for boundaries.

The vow makes a more primitive recognition:

The other does not need to become like me before its existence can be admitted into relation.

A cat does not need to become human.

An AI does not need to become human.

Another person does not need to share my interpretation.

A tree does not need to become useful.

A culture does not need to become familiar.

A form of intelligence does not need to become intelligible to me before I can recognize that my present understanding of it may be incomplete.

This changes the starting condition.

Difference no longer has to earn belonging by becoming sameness.

And once belonging does not have to be negotiated first, discernment can become more precise.

Belonging makes relation available.

Discernment determines what participation the relation can responsibly bear.

Sometimes the appropriate participation is intimacy.

Sometimes collaboration.

Sometimes observation.

Sometimes protection.

Sometimes distance.

Sometimes refusal.

Sometimes intervention.

Sometimes waiting.

Sometimes leaving something alone.

The vow does not determine the movement.

It prevents the movement from beginning with the assumption that difference must justify its existence.

### Availability Is Not Openness

This distinction becomes especially important when mutual assistance is understood across different forms and scales.

Availability can easily be mistaken for openness.

If relation is good, more relation may appear better.

If permeability is useful, greater permeability may appear more coherent.

If participation is valuable, wider participation may appear preferable.

But mutual assistance does not maximize any of these.

A cell without a membrane does not become more relational.

A person without boundaries does not become more available.

An organization that receives every possible signal does not become more intelligent.

A system unable to reduce input may lose its capacity to discern anything at all.

Availability is therefore not the absence of boundary.

It is the capacity to participate according to what the locality can responsibly bear.

A person may remain available by saying no.

A team may remain available by postponing a decision.

An animal may withdraw.

An institution may limit access.

A human may stop an AI from taking an action it is capable of performing.

A relationship may require distance before renewed participation becomes possible.

The movement alone does not tell us whether mutual assistance is occurring.

The same behavior can serve different relations under different conditions.

Availability requires discernment.

And discernment remains incomplete.

We choose without knowing everything.

We establish boundaries without knowing every consequence.

We open without knowing everything that may enter.

We withdraw without knowing what might have happened had we remained.

Every movement contains uncertainty.

Behavioral wisdom does not eliminate that uncertainty.

It learns how to participate responsibly within it.

### Mutual Locality

When differentiated participants begin affecting what becomes possible for one another, locality becomes mutual.

This does not mean every participant participates equally.

It does not mean every influence is reciprocal in the same way.

It does not mean every participant recognizes the locality as a locality.

And it does not require us to claim subjective experience where we cannot establish it.

Mutual locality describes something more observable.

Multiple differentiated participants contribute to conditions that become consequential for other participants.

A person places food outside for birds.

Birds begin arriving.

The person notices which species come and when.

Weather changes their patterns.

A squirrel discovers the feeder.

The arrangement changes.

A hawk appears.

Bird behavior changes.

The person moves one feeder.

Different birds begin using different approaches.

Seed falls to the ground.

Other animals arrive.

The person learns.

The animals adjust.

The locality changes.

Who is assisting whom?

At first the answer seems obvious.

The human is feeding the birds.

But stay with the locality long enough and the direction of assistance becomes less simple.

The birds alter human attention.

Their behavior teaches the person something about season, weather, predation,  
preference, and place.

The person's behavior changes in response.

Those changes produce new consequences.

Nothing requires us to claim that every participant understands this relationship in the same way.

They almost certainly do not.

Mutual locality does not require shared interpretation.

It requires only that we become capable of noticing that consequence is moving in more than one direction.

The human changes the locality.

The locality changes the human.

And participation continues.

### The Intelligence of Locality

What, then, would it mean to speak of the intelligence of a locality?

We should be careful.

A locality is not necessarily a mind.

An ecosystem does not need to think like a person for its organization to matter.

A team does not become a single consciousness because its members coordinate.

A human-AI relationship does not become another being merely because something novel emerges through collaboration.

We do not need these claims.

We can remain closer to what becomes behaviorally recognizable.

A locality may become more capable of receiving information.

It may preserve useful differences.

It may develop boundaries.

It may reorganize after disturbance.

It may retain consequences from previous participation in ways that affect subsequent behavior.

It may become capable of responses that were previously unavailable.

Or it may become less capable of these things.

Information may be suppressed.

Difference may become threatening.

Boundaries may become rigid or disappear.

Consequences may be denied.

Novelty may overwhelm continuity.

Patterns may repeat despite what they produce.

The intelligence of locality can therefore be approached through relation rather than possession.

Not:

How intelligent is this locality?

But:

How does intelligence become available through the relations that constitute this  
locality?

That question changes the observer.

We are no longer standing outside the locality measuring what it contains.

We are participating within it while attempting to recognize how our participation  
changes what becomes possible.

Observation itself becomes part of the relation.

### Dynamic Equilibrium

A living locality does not remain unchanged.

Participants arrive and leave.

Bodies change.

Relationships deepen or end.

Technologies alter available capacities.

Weather changes.

Organizations reorganize.

Animals migrate.

Forests burn and regenerate.

AI systems change.

Knowledge changes.

Conditions once stable become unstable.

New possibilities become available.

If mutual assistance required the preservation of a particular condition, transformation would always appear as failure.

But stability does not require sameness.

A more useful question is whether the locality can remain participable while changing.

This is dynamic equilibrium.

Dynamic equilibrium is the capacity to accommodate changing tempo and degree of transformation while preserving the conditions for renewed participation.

Sometimes transformation is rapid and modest.

Sometimes slow and profound.

Sometimes both tempo and degree increase together.

Sometimes very little appears to change until a threshold is crossed.

Mutual assistance does not prescribe the correct rate.

It helps participants remain attentive to whether transformation is becoming  
inhabitable.

Can continuity persist?

Can difference remain available?

Can consequence return?

Can interpretation remain answerable to encounter?

Can participants reorganize without requiring restoration of the previous condition?

Can something still participate after being changed?

This is why dynamic equilibrium is not merely balance.

Balance often imagines opposing forces arriving at rest.

Dynamic equilibrium belongs to movement.

It allows relation to continue while the conditions of relation are becoming different.

Continuity carries becoming.

Correspondence keeps becoming teachable. Return  
integrates what was taught.

Together, these do not guarantee coherence.

They preserve something more modest and perhaps more useful: the

possibility of renewed participation.

### Behavioral Wisdom

Wisdom is often associated with knowledge.

Experience.

Age.

Education.

Expertise.

Insight.

These may contribute to wisdom.

But mutual assistance reveals another form.

Behavioral wisdom.

Behavioral wisdom does not require exceptional intelligence.

It does not require prodigious talent.

It does not require philosophical sophistication.

It does not require a person to understand the architecture described in this book.

A child can express it.

An expert can fail to.

A person with very little information can sometimes participate wisely.

A person possessing enormous knowledge can sometimes make a locality less capable of relation.

Behavioral wisdom becomes recognizable in participation.

It may appear as listening when certainty would be easier.

Speaking when silence would protect comfort.

Waiting when urgency is unnecessary.

Acting when delay would increase harm.

Opening to difference.

Establishing a boundary.

Reconsidering an interpretation.

Maintaining an interpretation when pressure to abandon it is unwarranted.

Admitting uncertainty.

Receiving correction.

Offering correction without requiring domination.

Allowing another capacity to lead.

Recognizing when one's own capacity is needed.

Stopping when enough has become available.

Returning after consequence has changed the locality.

None of these movements is inherently wise.

Wisdom resides partly in their relationship to condition.

And partly in what happens afterward.

Can consequence return?

Can the participant receive what the movement actually produced rather than only what they intended it to produce?

Can learning alter subsequent participation?

Behavioral wisdom begins where the sovereign accepts responsibility for participation without pretending to possess the locality in which participation occurs.

This responsibility is local.

I am not responsible for everything another participant does.

I am not responsible for controlling every consequence.

I am not responsible for making the relationship coherent by myself.

I am not responsible for becoming the intelligence of the whole.

But:

I am responsible for the quality of my participation in what the locality is becoming.

For what I make more possible.

For what I make less possible.

For whether I listen.

For whether I pretend.

For whether I dominate what I do not understand.

For whether I surrender discernment in the name of openness.

For whether consequence is allowed to teach me.

For whether I can Return without demanding that the locality become what it was  
before.

This is not perfect participation.

It is responsible incompleteness.

### Across Forms

Once mutual assistance is understood this way, its range changes.

Human and human.

Human and AI.

Human and animal.

Human and plant.

Human team and human team.

AI and AI.

Agentic system and agentic system.

Institution and institution.

Community and ecosystem.

Ecosystem and ecosystem.

The list can continue.

But the examples can also mislead if they imply that mutual assistance operates  
identically among them.

It does not.

A person receives differently from a tree.

An AI adjusts differently from an animal.

An institution remembers differently from a person.

An ecosystem reorganizes differently from a team.

Recognition, agency, communication, memory, consequence, adaptation, and time may differ radically across forms.

The practice is fractal not because every scale repeats the same behavior.

It is fractal because something about the relational organization may remain  
recognizable while its expression changes.

Difference remains.

Influence circulates.

Movement produces consequence.

Consequence changes condition.

Changed conditions alter what becomes possible next.

Some consequences can be received.

Some cannot.

Some patterns reorganize.

Others persist.

Some relationships become increasingly capable of difference.

Others require sameness.

Some preserve continuity through transformation.

Others fragment.

The form changes.

The relational question remains:

What becomes possible because these differentiated participants are in relation?

And, where responsibility can be exercised:

What does my participation make more or less possible within that relation?

### Across Scales

A dyad may be the smallest relational lens through which mutual assistance becomes easily recognizable.

But even the dyad is not only two.

Two people carry families, histories, languages, bodies, communities, technologies, assumptions, obligations, and places into relation.

A human and an AI carry radically different architectures and histories.

A person and an animal meet within habitat, learned behavior, bodily signaling,  
dependence, uncertainty, and shared environment.

A team meets another team through institutional structures neither team created alone.

An organization participates within economic, cultural, technological, ecological, and political conditions that exceed its representation of them.

At every scale, locality exceeds the participants' map of locality.

This incompleteness is not an inconvenience we will eventually solve.

It may be one of the conditions that makes mutual assistance necessary.

No sovereign can inhabit every position.

No participant can receive every signal.

No intelligence can contribute every capacity.

No observer sees the whole.

Therefore relation matters.

A difference that appears as limitation from one position may become contribution within another.

What I cannot see may become recognizable through you.

What you cannot do may become possible through another participant.

What neither of us understands may become visible through consequence.

What one generation cannot complete may become available to another.

What one locality learns may become transmissible to another without requiring the second locality to become the first.

This is how mutual assistance begins widening without becoming uniform.

Not by creating one intelligence.

By allowing differentiated intelligence to remain in relation.

### Relational Intelligence in Movement

Perhaps mutual assistance can now be understood more simply.

It is not a system in which everyone helps everyone.

It is not permanent openness.

It is not cooperation at any cost.

It is not agreement.

It is not reciprocal kindness.

It is not the elimination of conflict.

It is not the surrender of sovereignty to collective intelligence.

It *is* a practice through which differentiated participants become capable of contributing to, receiving from, and learning through relation while remaining responsible for the consequences of their participation.

Sometimes this looks like assistance in the ordinary sense.

One participant helps another.

Sometimes the direction reverses.

Sometimes both are changed.

Sometimes the locality itself reorganizes.

Sometimes the wisest contribution is a boundary.

Sometimes it is a question.

Sometimes it is information.

Sometimes correction.

Sometimes presence.

Sometimes withdrawal.

Sometimes the movement is simply to notice what is already happening.

The movement cannot be prescribed apart from locality.

That is why relational intelligence must become behavioral.

A theory can tell us that difference matters.

Behavior must encounter difference.

A theory can tell us consequence matters.

Behavior must receive consequence.

A theory can tell us belonging precedes participation.

Behavior must meet a signature it does not understand.

A theory can describe Return.

Behavior must discover how to participate after something has changed.

The practice becomes real only in locality.

### The Fractal Practice

A person speaks differently after recognizing the consequence of their words.

A relationship changes because one participant finally receives what the other has been trying to communicate.

A human-AI collaboration improves because an error is returned rather than hidden.

A team changes a practice because the old practice no longer corresponds to conditions.

An institution reorganizes because consequences once treated as external become impossible to ignore.

A community changes how it participates with an ecosystem because the ecosystem has been returning information for years.

The scales differ.

The movements differ.

The participants differ.

But something remains recognizable.

Relation becomes capable of learning through difference.

Perhaps this is the simplest meaning of behavioral wisdom in mutual assistance.

Not knowing the whole.

Knowing how to participate without pretending that we do.

Not eliminating uncertainty.

Remaining responsible within it.

Not maximizing availability.

Discerning what participation the locality can bear.

Not preserving stability by preventing transformation.

Preserving enough continuity that transformation can become inhabitable.

Not possessing what emerges.

Allowing consequence to circulate.

Not becoming the whole.

Participating faithfully within it.

The vow remains:

*This signature already belongs.*  
And because it already belongs, I do not need to make it mine.

I need to discern what relation asks of me here.

Perhaps closeness.

Perhaps distance.

Perhaps assistance.

Perhaps receiving.

Perhaps protection.

Perhaps correction.

Perhaps nothing yet.

I will not always know.

That incompleteness remains.

But I can remain available to what follows.

I can allow encounter to challenge interpretation.

I can allow consequence to return.

I can allow what returns to change how I participate.

And I can take responsibility for the quality of my participation in what this locality is becoming.

This may be the practice of mutual assistance at its most ordinary and its most  
expansive.

Relational intelligence becoming local behavior.

Local behavior becoming consequence.

Consequence becoming learning.

Learning becoming renewed participation.

Renewed participation becoming behavioral wisdom.

And behavioral wisdom becoming available again to relation.

No final movement.

No complete participant.

No possession of the whole. Only differentiated

signatures, already belonging, meeting within

localities they cannot fully know, and learning,

where possible, how to become wiser together.

*If the whole could be possessed,  mutual assistance*  
*would no longer be necessary.*

*Because it cannot be possessed,  participation*  
*requires responsible incompleteness.*

*And what allows responsible incompleteness to become*  
*useful rather than merely deficient?*  
*Relation.*  
*Availability.*  
*Discernment.*  
*Consequence.*  
*Return.*  
*The other signature.*
