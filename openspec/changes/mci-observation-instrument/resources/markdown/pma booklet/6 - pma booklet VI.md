# VI.

## A Simple Practice

You do not need to memorize a sequence.

You do not need to perform Mutual Assistance correctly.

You can begin with an actual relationship.

When something happens, notice:

What am I encountering?

Then:

What am I recognizing?

These are not necessarily the same.

Something happened.

You interpreted what happened.

Your interpretation may correspond closely to the encounter.

It may not.

You may not yet know.

Remain teachable.

Then ask:

What participation can this relation responsibly bear?

Listen.

Discern.

Move.

Perhaps you speak.

Perhaps you wait.

Perhaps you ask.

Perhaps you offer.

Perhaps you refuse.

Perhaps you investigate.

Perhaps you continue.

Perhaps you withdraw.

Perhaps you recognize another movement entirely.

Then live what follows.

This part informs.

Mutual Assistance does not end when you make your movement.

Your movement enters a locality already containing other movements.

Something changes.

Maybe very little.

Maybe more than you expected.

Notice what follows without immediately converting consequence into verdict.

An uncomfortable consequence does not automatically mean the movement was wrong.

A pleasant consequence does not automatically mean it was wise.

Consequence is information before it becomes judgment.

Can you receive it?

Can you allow what happened to become available to your next participation?

This is Return.

Return does not mean restoring the relationship to what it was.

Sometimes what it was should not be restored.

Return means that what happened is allowed to participate in what happens next.

You return changed.

The other may return changed.

The locality may have changed.

And participation begins again.

Perhaps the practice can be held this simply:

Recognize.

Discern.

Participate.

Receive. Return.

Not stages to perfect.

Movements to notice.

### The Movement of the Practice

Vow gives orientation.

Discernment chooses within actual conditions.

Participation makes movement local.

Consequence returns information from participation.

Return makes consequence available to renewed participation.

Becoming is the lived maturation through these movements.

Behavioral wisdom becomes recognizable as that maturation increasingly informs  
behavior.

These are not stages of achievement.

They are movements that may recur, overlap, reverse, disappear, and become recognizable again within locality.
