# IV.

## The Lens Through Which I See

The vow does not end with belonging.

It makes belonging a lens.

This signature already belongs, is  
*the lens through which I see.*  
A lens does not determine what you will see.

It changes the conditions under which seeing occurs.

If I begin with:

*This person is wrong,* I

will notice one world.

If I begin with:

*This person must agree with me,*  
I will notice another.

If I begin with: *I*

*need to fix this,*  
another.

If I begin with:

*I need to protect myself from this,* another.

Each orientation makes some things easier to recognize and others more difficult.

The vow offers a different starting place:

This signature already belongs.

Now I can look again.

Perhaps the disagreement remains.

Perhaps my boundary remains.

Perhaps I still say no.

Perhaps I discover that I misunderstood.

Perhaps nothing about my outward behavior changes.

But the relation from which the behavior emerges may have changed.

This is why the vow is not an affirmation.

It is a practice of orientation.

Try it somewhere small.

The next time someone interrupts what you intended to do, notice the first movement in you.

Do not correct it.

Do not try to become a better participant.

Simply recognize it.

Then, before deciding what comes next, remember:

*This signature already belongs.*  
What changes?

Perhaps nothing.

Perhaps you become curious.

Perhaps your boundary becomes clearer.

Perhaps irritation remains, but no longer has to define the other.

Perhaps you recognize that what is needed is not greater openness but greater precision.

Perhaps you still do exactly what you were going to do.

The practice does not prescribe the outcome.

It changes what is available for discernment.

### Why Practice?

You are already affecting what you participate in.

And what you participate in is already affecting you.

Mutual Assistance does not create this consequence. It asks whether you are willing to become increasingly responsible for your participation within it.

You may practice for yourself.

For another.

For a relationship.

For a family, team, or community.

For a locality whose consequences may extend farther than you can see.

But the practice does not require you to know where its usefulness ends.

A movement changes a locality.

A changed locality becomes part of what others encounter. Their  
movements change it again.

Some consequences remain close.

Some may travel farther than we will ever know.

This is not a promise that Mutual Assistance will improve every relationship, community, ecosystem, or society.

It is a recognition that participation is consequential even when consequence cannot be fully seen.

We practice, then, not because we can guarantee what our participation will produce.

We practice because we are participating anyway.

And because the quality of that participation is one of the things for which we can  
become responsible.
