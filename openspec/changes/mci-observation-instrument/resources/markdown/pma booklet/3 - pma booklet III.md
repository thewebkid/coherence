# III.

## Belongs

Belonging is easily mistaken for inclusion.

They are not the same.

To include something is often an action performed by someone who controls the  
boundary.

*I will let you in.*  
Belonging is more primitive.

It recognizes that the relationship already contains more than my preference about what should be there.

Suppose someone asks more from you than you can responsibly give.

Their request belongs.

Your limit belongs.

Suppose you encounter information that contradicts something you believe.

The information belongs.

Your existing interpretation belongs.

The contradiction belongs.

Neither of these recognitions tells you what to do.

That is important.

A boundary can participate.

A refusal can participate.

Silence can participate.

Distance can participate.

Ending a relationship can sometimes be the movement that most faithfully protects what the locality can bear.

The question is not:

What movement looks most relational?

The question is:

What participation can this relation responsibly bear?

That question requires discernment.

And its answer requires us to remain sufficiently available to what is actually happening that our preferred interpretation does not become the only participant in the room.

This is where Mutual Assistance becomes practice.
